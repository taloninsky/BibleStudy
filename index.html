  • Others who may have seen the scroll:


    • The scroll was written on the front and the back, like Ezek 2:9-10, which says that there were written on it words of lamentation, mourning and woe.

Just a quick note, it is very interesting what God says in Is 29:13, that “their fear of Me is a commandment taught be men,” indicating that a true fear of the Lord is taught be the Lord (either formally or experientially, I assume).

    • Jeremiah signs and seals title document, with things on the inside and the outside that in Jeremiah 32:6-15, might be a type of prototype of this action, some have suggested that the scroll in question was a title deed for the future (since this is right before an invasion).

    • The temptation of Jesus by Satan in Matt 4:8-10, Satan’s tempts Jesus by a shortcut of not having to “pay” for the deed, and that he would just “give” it to Him, if Jesus worshiped Satan.


  • Military elements

    • Because the Lord loves us and is our friend: Psalm 25:14, Daniel 9:22-27, Daniel 10:21, John 15:15

Revelation 5:6 “And between the throne and the four living creatures and among the elders I saw a Lamb standing, as though it had been slain, with seven horns and with seven eyes, which are the seven spirits of God sent out into all the earth.”


  • Between the throne and the four living creatures: I think this points to the submission before the Father, in being before the throne, but still between the four living creatures and the throne, indicating that all the persons of the trinity are present and accounted for: The 7 torches burning, which are the 7 spirits of God, the One sitting on the throne (The ancient of days) and the Lamb (the Son, Jesus). They are all members of the Godhead. They are all focal points of worship. This made me think more about the nature of the trinity, so here are some passages that I’ve been thinking about:


    • He is God and He is with God and the Spirit: John 1:1-2,14, John 10:30, Col 1:15-16, John 14:9-11, Rev 22:1, Matt 28:19,

    • But, He works to glorify the Father, and by the Father’s authority: Phil 2:9-11, John 14:9-11,

    • He is referenced separately: Rev 5:13, Rev 1:4-5, John 14:16-17, Eph 1:3-14, 2 Cor 13:14,

    • But there is unity: John 17:21, Heb 1:3, Matt 3:16-17


  • Interestingly, the Lamb is both between the 4 creatures and the throne as well as among the 24 elders. This is a bit of a challenge to draw out spatially, when you think about it. In my head, the throne is in the center, the torches are before it, the Lamb is with the torches before the throne, the four living creatures are on four sides of the throne and the 24 elders are surrounding the 4 living creatures. This puts Jesus on both sides of the 4 living creatures at the same time, if I have that right. But, that also would point to the fact that Jesus is both God and man. He is both firstborn among many brethren and one with the Father. He is the first and the last. This is a superposition that just feels impossible. Not just spatially, but even in terms of justice. How can God take our sins on Himself?? This so beyond my comprehension.


    • With God, Is God, and Flesh: John 1:1,14, Col 2:9,

    • He became sin and in humility and meekness, conquered sin, and now is clothed in righteousness and strength: Isaiah 53:12, Gen 1:26-27

    • We are made in God’s image Gen 1:26-27, after His likeness. How interesting that He would project himself perfectly from His original perfection (see Heb 1:3) into the the fallen line, so that in order to save Mankind for Himself and from themselves, He Himself would have to become one of them, how mind-bending! Rom 1:3-4, Heb 4:15, Heb 2:14-17, 2 Cor 5:21, Rom 8:3, Gal 4:4-5, Matt 1:1-16,

    • The craziness is that He was God, was in the form of God, but “let go” of equality with God. Phil 2:6-7, Is 9:6

    • He is fully man, fully mediator between God and Man, and fully God: 1 Tim 2:5, Rom 8:3, Matt 1:23

    • He remains in our midst and in the midst of the throne to guide us :Rev 7:17


  • “As though it had been slain.” This is really interesting. This is the new perfected body of Christ, the Lion of Judah, who will rule, and yet He appears to John as though He had been slain. Certainly this points to the sacrificial nature of Christ’s life and death, I wonder if it might also point to the eternal marks of His sacrifice...


    • the Greek word is ‘esphagmenon’ which translates to ‘had been slain’ (other translations slaughtered). Related words which have the same root are ‘Slaughter’, ‘massacre’, ‘Victim’, ‘sacrifice’. The Greek form indicates a completed action with ongoing effects. Obviously, His sacrifice has the ongoing effect of our redemption, but I also wonder about the ongoing effects on the Lamb. The text does say “...standing as if it had been slain...” so it is alive and upwardly mobile. This is not a surprise, but I’m exploring the literal text. This is related to His mediating work (Heb 7:23-25). He can continue to intercede because 1) His sacrifice was perfect and complete, 2) because He rose from the dead (He is standing).

    • He took the punishment, willingly, without complaint, for us all, and he bears those scars: Is 53:5-7, 1 Peter 1:18-19, Rev 13:8, John 20:27, Zech 12:10, Rev 1:7, Rev 19:13,

    • It is not an ongoing punishment, it is completed: Heb 9:12,


  • With Seven Horns: This points to Jesus’ Omnipotence, as horns are symbols of power and He is perfect in power (7 horns).


    • Perfect in power: Dan 7:14, Matt 28:18, Heb 1:3, Rev 1:8, Rev 19:15-16,

    • In submission to the Father: Ps 2:8-9

    • Horns as symbols of power: Deut 33:17,

    • Jesus having horns: 1 Sam 2:10, Zechariah 1:18-19,

    • 7 as a number of completion in creation: Gen 2:2-3, Ex 20:10-11

    • 7 as a number of completion in worship and ritual: Lev 22:15-16, Lev 4:6, Lev 25:8,

    • 7 as a number of completion in judgement and fulfillment: Josh 6:4, 2 Kings 5:10,

    • 7 in prophecy: Dan 9:24, Zech 4:10, Rev 1:4, Rev 5:1, Rev 8:2, Rev 15:1, Rev 16:17,

    • 7 as perfection and fulfilment: Ps 12:6, Pr 9:1, Matt 18:21-22


  • having seven eyes: I believe these eyes related to the omniscience of the lamb, having full knowledge and awareness of those things around Him and everywhere. This might seem counterintuitive, since the 4 creatures of covered in eyes (one would think that more than 7 eyes are better than 7 eyes), but I don’t think that is what the symbolism points to. Being covered with eyes (I believe) points to the creatures “high dimensional” or amplified or highly-triangulating awareness and/or knowledge. They need that to be able to optimally worship and observe the Ancient of Days. In contrast to that, the Lamb has 7 eyes, as already discussed, that is the completion or the perfection of knowledge, speaking toward omniscience, rather than high awareness.

      • I think that we are seeing the legal specificity of Romans here. Justification is a concept of being declared righteous from to past events. This is the present reality.

      • Salvation from wrath, which is a future event, Paul speaks to reassure believers ‘how much more shall we be saved’ so it is not that the events are unrelated, but rather that the events are separated in time. The wrath is yet to come (mostly detailed in Revelation, but oft referred to as ‘the day of the LORD’)

      • Theologically, the sequence is 1) past tense: we have been saved us from the penalty of sin (justification), 2) present tense: we are being saved from the power of sin (sanctification), 3) future tense: we will be saved from the presence of sin (glorification).

      • The one-time event of Justification: Rom 5:1, Eph 2:8-9, Rom 3:23-24. The continuous process of Sanctification: Phil 2:12-13, 2 Cor 3:18, Gal 5:16, Rom 5:3-5, James 1:2-4, 1 Peter 4:12-13, Phil 3:10, Heb 12:5-11, 1 Peter 1:6-7, Heb 5:8-9, Rom 6:5-6, 2 Cor 1:8-9, Matt 5:10-12. The eternal (ongoing forward) adventure of Glorification: Rom 8:30, 1 John 3:2, Phil 3:20-21, Rev 21:4.

      • Put another way: we have been justified by Christ’s blood, we are being sanctified by our suffering and by the grace of the Holy Spirit and we will be glorified with Christ and present with the LORD to rule in heaven as a kingdom of priests in His presence.

      • Apparently, from what I’m reading, there are opinions on both sides of the aisle: that we will be glorified in an instant or that we will be glorified over the extent of eternity. The event side scriptures: Rom 8:30, Phil 1:6, Phil 3:20-21, 1 Cor 15:51-52, 1 John 3:2, Col 3:4. The ongoing process side scriptures: 2 Cor 3:18, Rom 8:18,

Revelation 5:12 “saying with a loud voice, ‘Worthy is the Lamb who was slain, to receive power and wealth and wisdom and might and honor and glory and blessing!’ ”


  • "Worthy” is ‘Axion’ in Greek. Having definitions: worthy, deserving, valuable, proper, in keeping with or corresponding to what is expected. These English words are made from it:


    • Axiom - which is often used in philosophical or mathematical contexts to mean “self-evident truth” or “that which is thought worthy or fit”

    • Axiomatic - obviously similar to Axiom, but further considered universally accepted without needing further explanation. As in, ‘assumed to be true’.

    • Axis - think of the x axis and the y-axis from when you learned to graph a line in math class. though primarily derived from the latin word axis, meaning “pivot” or “axle”, it is also derived from the Greek “worthy” or “fitting”

    • Axial - related to axis, but also having the further implications, such as in of a scientific or mechanical context to describe structures or movements related to a central axis… such as the axial vs radial movements of a tire on a car. It points to centrality.


  • “is” - this is present tense. It is a tensed verb in English, but even more in Greek. In Greek it is the third-person, singular. It connects the object (the Lamb) with its current quality or state (worthy). It is also in the active voice, which means the object (the lamb) is actively performing the action of being or existing in this state. In Greek, there is a quality called “Aspect” and this verb has the imperfect aspect, meaning the action or state is continuous or ongoing. i.e. the Lamb continues to be worthy.

  • “who was slain” ~~~> in Greek, the words do not exist for “who was” so a more literal translation reads something like “Worth is the Lamb slain”. Looking into more of the Greek implication, the form of the word slain is “to esphagmenon” which is described as the perfect passive participle meaning “having been slain” or “who was slain”. Perfect Tense means that action or event occured in the past, but has ongoing significant or results in the present. Passive voice, the Lamb is the one who received the action of being slain, not the one performing the action. i.e. it was not a suicide. Lastly, it is a participle, so it specifies why the Lamb is considered worth --> because of the perfection of the Lamb and His sacrifice, that is what makes Him worthy.
