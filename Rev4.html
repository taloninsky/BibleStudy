  • Rev 4:3


    • "And He who sat there had the appearance of jasper and carnelian,"


      • Ex 28:20, Ex 39:13 The 12 stones were part of the priestly breastplate of judgment, worn by the high priest, sardius/carn. Was first in the first row, jasper was last in the last row

      • Eze 28:11-19 King of Tyre lament related to creation, arrogance, fall, beautiful and perfect coverings

      • jasper - a precious stone, associated with vivid and varied hues… richly colored. solid/opaque, lustrous finish

      • Re 21:11 like a Jasper clear as crystal

      • Re 21:18 like clear glass

      • Re 21:19 the first foundation of the wall

      • carnelian/sardius is the first stone mentioned in Ex 28:20, and associated with the first tribe, which is Reuben).

      • According to rabbinic tradition the jasper stone was representative of the tribe of Benjamin which was the youngest or last son of Israel, it is the last stone mentioned (3rd position, 4th row in Ex 28:20)

      • carnelian/sardius - translucent, often prized for it warm, fiery tones, like the sun. Its translucence would allow light to through, giving it a luminous, almost living appearance

      • overall: vibrant life, fiery, rich, precious

      • Re 22:20 carnelian was 6th in the foundation of the wall

      • There may be a notion of the first and the last going here


    • "and around the throne was a rainbow that had the appearance of an emerald."


      • Ge9.12-17

      • emerald - green, precious, life, mercy

      • I think this signifies God's commitment to life, his love and everlasting covenant with us

      • I also think this hearken back to his authorship and creator-ship in creation.

      • Ezekiel 1:26-28 - Zeke's vision also includes the bow, and other things that we can cross-reference, interestingly, in his vision, the likeness of a throne was “in appearance like sapphire,” which is blue. That is also the 2nd row, 2nd stone, the tribe of Issachar. From different vantage points or maybe at different times, all three primary colors (red, green, blue), which might point back to the rainbow effect having to do with color.

      • The Greek word for rainbow is Iris, but I wonder if there is a sense of the shape and less of the color. Three words earlier it uses the word “around” or “encircling” or “surrounding” which are also geometrically contextual adjectives, so it seems like the throne was the central focus, this thing which was similar in sense to a rainbow surrounded it, but had the “appearance” or “was similar” or “resembling” an emerald. It was not simply green, but jewel-like.

      • Perhaps it was the “aura” or “emanating” effect coming from the one on the throne

      • is also mentioned as the last stone in the 1st row in Exodus 28:20, corresponding to the tribe of Levi

      • Rev 5:10 - a kingdom and and priests to our God, and they shall reign on the earth

      • I think the representat the governing body for Israel (12) and for the church (12)

      • I think it is also important to note that they are given the rank of "elder" which I believe is representative that we will rule with God (under God, but over some other creatures) - 1 Tim 2:12, Rev 5:10, Rev 20:4, Rev 22:5, Matt 19:28, Rom 8:17,

      • apparently, not just angles and the earth, but the kingdoms under the whole heaven”: Dan 7:27

  • Rev 4:6


    • "and before the throne there was as it were a sea of glass, like crystal."


      • sea of glass


        • calm, at peace

        • glass is transparent - no hidden terror (contrast with Ge1.2 "The earth was without form and void, and darkness was over the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters.")


      • crystal - clear, pure, radiant, ability to reflect light with brilliance, sparkle, dazzling, beautiful

      • Looking forward, in Rev 15:2, we have see another sea of glass (or the same one) glowing with fire...

      • There is a “sea” at the temple, called the “Bronze Sea” or the “Molten Sea” 1 Kings 7:23-26, 2 Chron 4:2-5, which was capable of holding about 12,000 gallons, used for ritual purification of the priests (Ex 30:17-21), it rested upon 12 bronze oxen, 4 groups of 3, each group facing a cardinal direction. I’m not sure if they are related.

      • A similar scene was observed by Moses in Ex 24:9-10, at Sinai, where is says that “...and they saw the God of Israel; and under His feet there appeared to be a pavement of sapphire, as clear as sky itself.”

      • strangely similar, Ezekiel saw in Eze 1:22, “Spread out above the heads of the living creatures was what looked something like a vault, sparkling like crystal and awesome.”


    • "And around the throne, on each side of the throne are four living creatures,"


      • If they are similar in size to the ‘model’ or ‘pattern’ that was given to Solomon for the Cherubim, They are about 15-17 feet tall with a similar wingspan. (1 Kings 6:23-28)

      • I believe that these are a specific kind of angelic being, like a seraphim (Is 6:1-7) or cherubim (Eze 1:4-14, Eze 10:1-22). Although they have 6 wings and 1 face, (unlike cherubim) and they are not burning like seraphim.

      •  The descriptions are kind of combined forms from Ezekiel (Eze 1:4-14, Eze 10:1-22) and from Isaiah (Is 6:1-7), and from the "pattern" from the tabernacle (Ex 25:18-22), and from 1st temple (1 Kings 6:23-28)

      • It is interesting that either cherubim or seraphim or similar creatures are near him and defending critical resources, 2 Sam 22:11-12, Gen 3:24, Num 7:89, 1 Sam 4:4, 1 Ch 13:6, Ps 80:1, Ps 99:1, Is 37:16, Eze 41:18-20, Heb 9:5

      • I think that the differences in the way that they are represented have something to do with the context that they are perceived, and perhaps the symbolism that is intended. I do not believe that they are symbolic though. I think they are real creatures, designed specifically to be able to minister to God Almighty, to fly near his brilliance, having the strength to worship in that proximity. It is hard to say though, there is so much symbolic language, but are all of the visions supposed to be symbolic? My hunch is that some things are literal.


    • "full of eyes in front and behind"


      • I think that this part might be literal, but is more likely a symbolic of their reality in way that we can understand. I think it represents their intense awareness of reality, their attention and observational faculties. No one will "sneak up" on these creatures.

      • I think this speaks of their ability to observe the Lord and drink in his beauty and worship him through observation. To a far lesser extent, a man adores a woman through looking at her, and a woman welcome and accepts that act and often works to be “worthy” of the gaze. I think God gave us this partly so that we can understand better.

      • David asks one thing of the Lord: “That I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to gaze upon the beauty of the Lord and to inquire in His temple.” Ps 27:4 So having lots of eyes and being nearby to the Lord is a dream come true.

      • Ezekiel’s vision also had eyes covering: Eze 1:18, Eze 10:12.

      • Rev 4:8 continues about the eyes

      • The notion of observing or admiring God is so rampant, it’s like sucking on a firehydrant… Here are some passages that point in this direction (and this is by no means exhaustive): Is 45:22, Ps 34:4-5, Heb 12:2, Is 40:9, Jn 1:29, Ps 123:1-2, Zech 9:9, Rev 3:20, 1 Chron 16:11, Mic 7:7, Ps 27:8, Num 21:8-9, Jn 3:14-15, Ps 27:4, Is 33:17, Ps 16:8, Is 26:3, 2 Cor 3:18, Ps 5:3, Is 17:7, Ps 121:1-2, Is 65:1, Ps 46:8, Ps 63:2, Ex 14:13, Is 25:9, Ps 66:5, 2 Chron 7:14, Ps 27:8, Is 31:1, Ps 105:3-5, Ps 24:6, Matt 5:8, Rev 22:4, Ps 17:15, Acts 7:55-56, Col 3:1-2, 2 Cor 3:16, Job 19:25-27, Ps 5:3, Jn 1:36,



  • Rev 4:7


    • "the first living creature like a lion"


      • Typically strength, fierce, threatening, power, courage, royalty, kingship, danger, destruction, judgement, protection, majestic



    • "the second living creature like an ox"


      • Typically strength, labor, service, humility, sacrifice, provision, blessing, authority, leadership, stubbornness, rebellion


    • "the third living creature with the face of a man"


      • chief among creatures, image of God, dominion, stewardship, fallenness and sinfulness, mortality and frailty, wisdom and understanding, relationship with God, dependence on God


    • "the fourth living creature like an eagle in flight"


      • strength, power, speed/swiftness, protection, care, renewal and longevity, divine judgement, majesty/nobility, spiritual vision and perception

      • Ex 19:4 (I bore you on eagle’s wings)


    • Overall, I believe that the differences pertain to God's perfect plan that is not cookie-cutter. These creatures are either separate in their purpose and capability or we are glimpsing 4 aspects of each of their capabilities (like Ezekiel).   

    • It is interesting that there are a lot of shared features and a lot of unique features among these symbols.


  • Rev 4:8


    • "And the the four living creatures, each of them with six wings, are full of eyes all around and within"


      • I believe the 6 wings hearkens back to Isaiah's description, that the wings provided not only flight, but also humility in covering their feet, etc. since where they reside is holy

      • it is interesting that in Isaiah's vision, with 2 wings they covered their eyes, but in John's vision, they could not cover their eyes… there were covered with eyes… I'm not sure what this means to us

      • I think that the eyes 'within' refers to the awareness that the creatures had of themselves, of their purpose, of the position, especially in relation to the Lord


    • "and day and night they never cease to say, 'Holy, holy, holy is the Lord God Almighty, who was and is and is to come.' "


      • Their focus is so poignant…


        • Holy, holy, holy - the absolute sacred superlative

        • Lord, God Almighty - the Greek is "Kyrios Theos Pantokrator" or Supreme Ruler, Supreme Being, Omnipotent One

        • Who was, Who is, who is to come - the existent One (I am, Yaweh), God's eternality and temporal/timelessness.


      • I just noticed that this is 3 groups of 3 concepts, which is a compound type of perfection.

      • this drives home that the eternality of God is a key part of His identity.



  • Rev 4:10


    • "The twenty-four elders fall down before Him who is seated on the throne and worship Him who lives forever and ever"


      • There is no doubt about which character is being worshiped here, nor any doubt that the point is they He is on the throne and that He is Eternal.


    • "they cast their crowns before the throne,"


      • I believe this is another indicator that these are saints, because their crowns are cast before the throne, knowing that all that have, even their rewards are not their own, since His will worked in their hearts and minds and intentions. It is all his, it is an indication that they were saved by Him.



  • Rev 4:11


    • "say, 'Worthy are you, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power, for You created all things, and by Your will they existed and were created.' "


      • I think that this is a slightly different tune than the creatures. No less true, but the focus is the worthiness of God.

      • 'Our Lord and God' is both introspective and corporate, I believe it is a joining in with the other angelic creatures, with a different phraseology.

      • reasoning and justifying: 'for You created all things, and by Your will they existed and were created.'

      • I think it is also important to point out here that what they are saying is that He is worthy for two notable reasons:


        • He created all things (therefore all things belong to Him, including glory and honor and power, along with all creatures He choses)

        • By His will they existed. In other words, he is worthy because we continue to exist. It is by the force of His will that we have our being and continue to have our being.

I believe that authorial intent of this chapter is our understanding that


  • We will be called up to be with the Lord

  • We can exist in His presence and look on Him without dying

  • God is on the highest throne in heaven

  • He is high and lifted up

  • His creatures are created for his purposes, with details that we may or may not be privy to understand, but He is willing that we should try, with some literal and some symbolic details given

  • We will reign with him in a literal sense, some greater than others, though I do not believe that the exact 24 identities are known to us.

  • Worship will be very a significant part of time and life for us in our immortality, I believe that worship will be our highest calling, but that we will likely have to take breaks, not because it is boring but because it is overwhelming and we might need to step away from the "heat" of His presence.

  • At least some of the saints are with Him "after these things," but before we are (obviously)

  • I think that this is one example of God's showing the temporal representation of our time in heaven, since we are not just "being" in nirvana, but we are taking actions, communicating, etc. all things that requrie our temporality.
